# Features

Ideas for what a cell (about 1 km across) can hold besides its land. Each cell rolls one major
feature from its biome's `features` list; today that is a settlement or a quarry. This is a
brainstorm, not yet a plan: a pool to pick from, weighed so the cheap, striking ones go first.

## Limits

- **Technology up to the first muskets**, around 1550. Water and wind mills, blast furnaces,
  printing, bastion forts and powder mills are in. Steam, rails and rifling are out.
- **Mostly made by people.** Magic is a flavour on top of that: towers, circles, shrines, odd
  stones. It is not a second world.
- **Every feature has a state.** The same kind can be brand new, worked, worn, abandoned, ruined or
  overgrown (see Cross-cutting). A ruin is usually a state of a feature, not a kind of its own.
  Only the truly ancient kinds below are ruins by nature.

## Cross-cutting

Any feature should be able to roll these, the way any biome rolls snow and relief.

| Parameter | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Condition** | new · worked · worn · abandoned · ruined · overgrown. Drives roof gaps, fallen walls, moss and dirt tints, rubble props, missing doors, and how far the foliage comes back into the clearing. Feeds the house generator as a decay amount. |
| **Style** | Whose it is: today's timber, stone and market-town styles, plus new ones the biomes want: **adobe** (deserts, red bush), **log** (taiga, steppe), **stilt and thatch** (wetlands, palm grove), **hide and felt** (nomads). The same feature kind in another biome's style is free variety. |
| **Size tier** | Like settlements' hamlet / village / town: a lone watchtower or a fortified line, a croft or an estate. |
| **Clearing** | Exists for features. Ruins get a smaller one, or one that is growing back. |

## Engine pieces

What the features above keep asking for, roughly by how much each unlocks.

1. **Compound layout.** A few buildings round a yard, a fence or wall about it, a gate toward the
   road, with no streets. Most residential, farming, industry, service and military kinds are
   this. The house generator and settlement placement already do most of the work.
2. **Condition / decay.** One number per feature, fed to the building generator and the props.
   Every kind gets a ruin twice over, for a single piece of work.
3. **Props.** Small authored meshes scattered by rules: barrels, crates, carts, log and plank
   stacks, hay ricks, fences, tents, hides on frames, boats, rubble. Needed almost everywhere.
4. **Terrain stamps beyond the quarry's cut**: a level pad; a mound (motte, barrow, charcoal
   heap); ring ditches and banks (hillforts, forts); terraces down a slope; a pit (clay, open
   mine); an adit (the quarry face with a hole); a pan or pond held level (salt works, mill pond).
5. **Fields.** Crop materials (wheat, barley, vines in rows, rice paddies, tea), field shapes split
   off the clearing, and boundaries between them: hedges, drystone walls, ditches.
6. **Waterside placement.** Find a river bank, a shore or a pool edge in the cell and face the
   feature to it. Mills, jetties, stilts, weirs and bridges all need it.
7. **Round and tall shapes.** Towers, windmills, yurts, lighthouses, and ring layouts (stone
   circles). Houses today are built from rectangular masses.
8. **Linear features.** Walls, aqueducts, beacon chains and old roads, which cross cells. This is
   the heaviest piece, and should come last.
9. **Effects.** Smoke (kilns, charcoal, chimneys), steam (hot springs), turning wheels and sails,
   glow and floating stones for magic.

## A possible first slice

The kinds that need the fewest new pieces, but that make the world feel lived-in quickly:

1. **Watchtower, wayside shrine, stone circle, waygate, barrows.** Single objects or rings on a
   hilltop, so they are mostly placement. They also prove "find a summit" placement.
2. **Compound layout** (piece 1), then the **homestead, logging camp, sheepfold, coaching inn and
   shieling**. Five biome families get something at once.
3. **Condition** (piece 2), which turns every kind above into an abandoned or ruined variant too.
4. **Mine** (the quarry's cut plus an adit), then **props** (piece 3).
5. After that, pick by biome: whichever family still feels emptiest gets its signature feature
   (caravanserai, stilt village, cliff dwellings, step-temple).

## What "done" means

A feature kind is finished when it clears all of these, the way each zone was finished before the
next one was started:

1. **Placement**: it finds ground that suits it in its cell. If nothing suits, the cell is left
   empty rather than given a bad fit. It keeps clear of roads, settlements and water.
2. **Ground**: the ground is shaped under it (levelled, cut or built up), painted with its own
   material, and cleared of plants round it. It gets a track to the road where one makes sense.
3. **Buildings and objects**: each is made by a generator from JSON5, in the biome's style, with a
   far level of detail and collision.
4. **Props**: enough small things that it looks used. No bare buildings on bare ground.
5. **Variety**: sizes and choices rolled per site, so two never look alike. Condition, once that
   exists.
6. **Biomes**: it is listed with odds in every biome it suits, and checked by walking through each
   of them.
7. **Workbench**: it can be previewed on its own, with every rolled option.
8. **Probe**: a Node check that placement and the ground shaping hold across many seeds.
9. **Map and teleport**: it shows on the debug map and can be teleported to.
10. **Load time**: measured, so startup stays fast.

## First: the logging camp

A camp working a stand of timber. Its parts:

- a bunkhouse and a saw shed round a trodden yard;
- logs stacked by length, a saw pit with a log on its trestles, a chopping block;
- a cut-over clearing of stumps all round, a few felled trunks still lying in it;
- its roads (one to the village, maybe one on to a sawmill), and a skid trail out into the felling.

It goes in the snowy taiga, the autumn wood and the bluebell wood. It is the first compound, so most
of the work is the general pieces, built properly once and then reused:

- features as places in the road network;
- the compound layout, with several entrances;
- props;
- the stumps;
- a ground stamp;
- a feature preview in the workbench.

### 1. Roads between places

Features are nearly as important to the roads as settlements. They are not hung off a road that
was laid between two villages.

**Today.** The network is planned over settlements only (roads/roadNetwork.ts):

1. A Delaunay triangulation gives the candidate pairs, dropping any longer than 5 km.
2. Shortest first, a spanning forest is taken, plus the shortest quarter of the leftovers for
   loops.
3. Each link is built shortest first: gates on both rims, an A* route (cheaper where a road
   already runs, so trunks are shared), then shaping and a graded height profile.
4. Access and rescue passes follow.
5. Last, each feature gets a dead-end spur to the nearest road.

**Places.** The plan is made over **places** instead: every settlement, and every feature whose
kind wants a road. Each place has:

| Field | Settlement | Feature (from its kind's `road` block) |
|---|---|---|
| position, radius | as now | its site and footprint |
| `importance` | by tier: town > village > hamlet | e.g. coaching inn about a village, logging camp about a hamlet, quarry below that |
| `links` (fewest, most) | at least 1, no limit | inn [2, 4] (never a dead end), camp [1, 2], quarry [1, 1] |
| gates | chosen while building, as now | the same: on its rim, facing the place each road goes to |
| `wants` | - | kinds it links to by preference: a mine to a smelter, a camp to a sawmill or a town, a summer pasture to the village under it |

**The plan, changed:**

1. **Candidates:** Delaunay over all places.
2. **Skeleton:** the spanning forest, shortest first, with each length divided by its ends'
   importance. Important places join first and most directly, and small ones hang off them.
3. **Link counts:** a place short of its fewest links takes its best remaining candidates. A place
   at its most takes no more.
4. **Loops:** still added, but preferring important pairs, so they form between towns and inns,
   not between two quarries.
5. **Wants:** a feature's wanted kinds cost less, so roads go somewhere that means something.
6. **Laid once, classed by traffic:** a route lays road only where there is none yet. Where it runs
   along an existing road, it adds its weight (its lesser place's importance) to that stretch's
   traffic. Stretches are split at every junction (roads/roadGraph.ts). A stretch's traffic sets
   its class (highway, road, track), which sets its width and surface. So where the routes between
   important places run together, the road they share is a highway. With twice the nodes, the
   countryside must not become a mesh of identical roads.

**Spurs stop being a special case.** A quarry is a place allowed one link, and a low place's link
may also end on the nearest road (a T-junction), as spurs do now.

**The rest of building is unchanged:** gates, A*, shared trunks, shaping, profiles, and the access
and rescue passes.

**The world's order** follows the settlements' own:

1. Settlements and compounds are sited, as a position and a radius.
2. Quarries cut their ground.
3. The network is planned and built over all places, choosing each one's gates.
4. Settlements grow their streets from their gates. Compounds lay out round theirs.
5. One late pad stage levels both.

So a compound's entrances are where its roads actually arrived, not a guess.

**Watch:**

- **Load time.** More nodes mean more routing. Measure it, and maybe give low links a smaller
  search budget.
- **Road density.** The controls are the link counts, the classes and the loop share, tuned on the
  debug map.
- **Build order.** Today it is shortest first, so long roads bundle onto short ones. With classes,
  trunks first with tracks joining them may be better. Try both.

### 2. The `compound` feature type

A new `type: "compound"` in `featureTypes.ts`, beside `quarry` and `settlement`, with its own block.
A compound is:

- a yard;
- buildings round it, by an arrangement rule;
- props placed by anchors;
- one or more entrances;
- optionally a boundary (a fence, palisade or wall), broken at the entrances;
- a clearing.

Nothing in it is specific to logging: the homestead, the sheepfold and the coaching inn are later
`features/*.json5` files of the same type.

**Arrangements**, named in the kind's file:

| Arrangement | How | For |
|---|---|---|
| `ring` | round the yard's rim, facing in, the entrances kept free | logging camp, sheepfold, summer pasture |
| `courtyard` | closing the yard on three or four sides, corners touching | farmstead, caravanserai, inn, monastery |
| `row` | in a line along the lane, facing it | trapper's camp, waystation |
| `scatter` | loosely spaced, turned freely | nomad camp, army camp |

Only `ring` is built for the logging camp. Each of the others is built with the first compound that
needs it, so there is something to test it against.

**Entrances**, each with a kind:

| Kind | What it joins | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `road` | a link in the places network; it opens at the gate the link arrived at | the camp's road to the village |
| `trail` | not a road: a narrow, ungraded path to `clearing`, `water`, `uphill` or `away`, ending there | the camp's skid trail into the felling |

A kind's `links` decide how many `road` entrances it gets. A coaching inn with [2, 4] links is a
through-stop by construction, not a special mode.

**Lanes:** between each pair of entrances, a lane crosses the yard. Props keep off it, and
buildings may face it (`doorTo: "lane"`) rather than the yard's middle (`doorTo: "yard"`).

**Boundary:** the format has it. Its generator waits for the first compound that needs one (a
farmstead, a stockade).

```json5
// features/loggingCamp.json5 (sketch)
{
  name: "Logging camp",
  type: "compound",
  compound: {
    site: { grade: [0, 0.12], cover: [0.5, 1], nearWater: 120 },  // gentle, in thick wood, near a river if it can be
    yard: { shape: "round", radius: [14, 22] },
    arrangement: "ring",
    entrances: [
      { kind: "trail", to: "clearing", length: [40, 90], chance: 0.8 },  // the skid trail; road entrances come from `road.links`
    ],
    buildings: [
      { building: "bunkhouse", count: [1, 2], doorTo: "yard" },
      { building: "sawShed", count: [1, 1], required: true },
      { building: "toolShed", count: [0, 1] },
    ],
    props: [
      { prop: "logStack", count: [3, 6], at: "yardEdge" },
      { prop: "sawPit", count: [1, 1], at: "yard" },
      { prop: "choppingBlock", count: [1, 2], at: "byBuilding", side: "door" },
      { prop: "felledLog", count: [4, 10], at: "trail" },      // along the skid trail
      { prop: "brushPile", count: [3, 8], at: "clearing" },
    ],
    boundary: null,
    clearing: { radius: [55, 90], stumps: true },  // the cut-over ground: trees become stumps
  },
  road: { importance: 1, links: [1, 2], wants: ["sawmill", "settlement"], class: "track" },
  ground: { material: "sawdustEarth", weight: { /* featureGap graph, like the quarry's */ } },
}
```

#### Siting (`compound.ts`, `evaluateCompound`)

Before the roads, as a position and a radius only. It works like `evaluateQuarry`, among the cell's
24 candidates:

- **Must have:** dry land, away from area borders, and a grade within `site.grade` over the
  compound's radius.
- **Scores:**
  - the ground is level across it;
  - tree cover is thick round it;
  - a river is near, when `nearWater` is set.
- **Tree cover** is read with `createTreeCover`, the same density the scatter uses. A camp in a
  thin wood scores low, and in open ground it is not placed at all.
- **Radius:** reserved from the yard's largest roll plus the ring of buildings, so the roads keep
  out of it.

#### Layout

After the roads, once per site, deterministic from its seed. It is planned only (no meshes), as
the settlement layout is:

1. **Entrances first.** Its `road` entrances are the gates its links arrived at. Its trails are
   aimed at their destinations. Their gaps on the rim are reserved, and the lanes between them
   drawn.
2. **The yard** is a level pad at the ground's mean height (at a gate's road height, where a road
   comes in), with a cut-and-fill margin back to the natural ground.
3. **Buildings,** by the arrangement, largest first. Each is measured with `measureBuilding`, then
   set where it:
   - keeps clear of the entrances, the lanes and the buildings already placed;
   - needs the least cut and fill under its own pad;
   - turns its door as `doorTo` asks.

   One that fits nowhere is left out. If a `required` one fits nowhere, the site fails.
4. **Props,** by anchor:
   - `yard`: inside, off the lanes;
   - `yardEdge`: round the rim, between the buildings;
   - `byBuilding`: against a building's door, wall or back;
   - `entrance`: either side of a gate;
   - `trail`: along a trail;
   - `clearing`: scattered in the cut-over ring, turned at random, sat on the slope.

   Each prop declares its footprint, so nothing overlaps.

#### Ground

The yard's pad and each building's pad join the late pad stage that settlements already use
(padField), so both are levelled the same way after the roads. Trails are drawn as narrow, nearly
ungraded road lines painted as trodden earth.

The feature field still reports, from the site:

- `featureGap` (0 inside the yard), so the `ground` material layer paints it;
- `featureClear`, from the clearing radius.

#### Drawing and collision

**Done:** `settlementRenderer.ts` became `buildings/buildingRenderer.ts`, a general renderer for
groups of placed buildings - a settlement's houses, a compound's buildings - owning shadows,
collision in and out by distance (per group), `floorAt` and cellar cuts. Each building is its own
mesh, near and far, shown and given its far detail by its own distance; nothing is merged. Props
will feed it too.

No second copy of that code.

### 3. Props

A new asset folder, `props/`, like `buildings/`. Each names a generator (`type`) with its settings
under its name, and produces the same `BuildingModel`, so it shares:

- the building materials and mesh;
- far detail (a prop's far model is simplified or left out past a distance of its own);
- `ColliderBuilder` collision. Small props have none.

The generators the camp needs, each low-poly and rolled per variant:

| Prop | Generator | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Log stack | `logStack` | Logs in a pyramid or a crib, between two stakes; ends showing a pale cut face with rings |
| Felled log | `log` | One trunk lying, its branches lopped, a root plate or a sawn end |
| Saw pit | `sawPit` | A trestle frame over a cut in the ground, a log on it, a long saw. Needs its own small ground cut (see Ground: a pit stamp) |
| Chopping block | `block` | A round of trunk, an axe in it, split wood heaped by it |
| Brush pile | `brush` | Lopped branches heaped; leaf-card branches from the tree generator's own atlas, so it matches the wood |
| Sawhorse, barrel, crate, cart | `boxes`-like | The general small stuff, reused by every later compound |

Materials needed:

- **Building materials:** `cutWood` (the rings) and `sawnPlanks`.
- **Ground material:** `sawdustEarth` (trodden earth with chips and sawdust).

### 4. Stumps where trees were cut

Today a feature's `featureClear` just removes trees. Inside a compound's `clearing.stumps`, the
scatter should keep each tree it would have grown there, but as a **stump**.

- **The model:** a short model made by the tree generator from the same trunk and roots: the trunk
  cut a little above the ground, with a pale top in the cut-wood material. Its kind and bark stay
  the tree's own, so a spruce stump looks like a spruce.
- **Placement:** cleared trees are only stumped inside the clearing ring, not on the yard.
- **Collision:** each stump's collision is its trunk and roots, cut to its height.

So the clearing reads as worked timber, not a plain lawn, and the stumps fall where real trees stood.

### 5. The camp's buildings

Log buildings, big enough inside (at the 2x scale) for a few people to a room. A log style the
taiga's settlements can use later too.

| Building | Size (tiles) | What it is | Inside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bunkhouse | 7-9 x 3 | long log hall, porch along the front, a lean-to along the back | three rooms at most: a mess hall, the bunk loft over it, a store in the lean-to; 2 exits |
| Cookhouse | 4-5 x 3 | log, a big stone chimney, porch | kitchen and mess hall; 1-2 exits |
| Saw shed | 4-6 x 3 | roof on heavy posts, open on two or three sides, one end closed | the open floor; the closed end a tool room |
| Ox stable | 3-6 x 3 | log or plank, wide double doors; its length rolled in stalls, a bay each, so it holds two oxen or six | stalls, feed store |
| Tool shed | 2 x 2 | a small log store | shut |

A camp rolls a bunkhouse and a saw shed (required), and a cookhouse, a stable or a tool shed by
chance.

**A new plan generator, `cabin`:**

- a long main block, with **lean-tos**: lower, single-sloped, along all or part of the back or a
  side (the `house` plan's wings are narrower blocks with gables of their own);
- **open sides**: a storey's walls left out on chosen sides, the roof carried on posts at every
  bay - the saw shed, and later a market hall, a cart shed;
- a **porch** on a chosen wall.

**New building parts:**

- **`logs` framing**: crossed, saddle-notched log ends standing out at every corner, a heavy sill
  log. The faces stay flat, in a new log wall material (horizontal logs and chinking, the courses
  in its bump), so only the corners are real logs and the triangle count stays low.
- **`leanTo` roof**: one slope against a taller wall - lean-tos and porches.
- **`porch`**: a lean-to roof on posts along a wall, a plank deck, a step or two; walkable under.
- **Braced door**: the framed door with a Z-brace of boards on its panel.

**New content:** materials (log wall, cut log end - rings, for log stacks and stumps later too -
rough boards) and room types with log insides (bunk room, mess room, foreman's room, tool room,
stalls).

**Order:**

1. The log wall material and the `logs` framing: **done.** The corners are square hewn beams, one
   course tall on the walls' course lines, their faces in the wall's plane and paint so the wall
   runs on into them; cut ends in end grain (a 2.8 m trunk's rings, each end off its heart by up
   to `endOffset`). Heights are counted in whole 0.6 m courses.
2. The `cabin` plan, lean-tos and the `leanTo` roof: **done.** A cabin's main block is two storeys
   (the hall and a low loft), always gabled; a lean-to is a one-storey wing against it, its pitch
   held under the wall it leans on.
3. The porch: **done.** A deck (deck boards), a step, posts and a beam, a lean-to roof open at its
   ends; walkable. A wall takes a porch or a lean-to.
4. Open sides: **done.** An `open` mass has no walls, posts along its outer edges about every
   `postEvery` bays, a boarded floor; a walled mass's wall inside its roof goes up to it. Not yet:
   a shed open on only some sides.
5. The interiors - the bunkhouse's first, to try the generator on a loft and a lean-to - then the
   cookhouse, the stable and the rest, swapped into the logging camp. Done so far: the saw shed
   (5-8 x 4 bays, a 30 degree roof) and the tool shed, without interiors; and the bunkhouse's
   interior: a mess hall filling the cabin's ground storey behind the front door, with a stair up
   through its ceiling to the bunk loft over it, open up to the roof (room types can now ask for
   that, `underRoof`), and a store in a lean-to - or at the cabin's end - with the second door out.
   A room can be held inside one block of the plan (`oneBlock`), so it never reaches from the cabin
   into a lean-to. The cookhouse: a one-storey cabin (4-5 x 3) open up to its roof, a mess hall
   behind the front door and a kitchen beside it, a big stone chimney on the kitchen's end (a room
   type's `hearth` draws the chimney to its wall), a porch most of the time, and half the time a
   back door into the kitchen. Open doorways are frames only - no leaves swung back in the way.
   Not yet: the hearth itself, inside (BACKLOG.md). The ox stable: a one-storey cabin 4-7 bays
   long, a barn door, a feed store a bay wide at one end and a stall to each other bay - three to
   six oxen - along its back wall: partitions, posts and a manger each, the aisle in front (a room
   type's `stalls`; a request's `through` keeps a room running front to back). The saw shed's tool
   room: the whole walled end, its one door opening onto the shed's floor (a door goes first in a
   wall onto an open part of the house).
   **Done:** all of them, and swapped into the logging camp - a bunkhouse and a saw shed always, a
   cookhouse, an ox stable and a tool shed by chance, round a yard of 18-24 m. The buildings being
   two or three times the stand-ins' size, the camp's circle grew (to 55-65 m), and its site was
   loosened to keep as many camps: a grade up to 0.2, and unevenness counted against its radius
   (10% of it) rather than a fixed 3 m. Its buildings stand 5 m back from the yard, and its
   circle reaches 14 m past their furthest corners, so a road arriving at a gate never grades
   their ground. The yard's level ground reaches out under the buildings' fronts - a wide one's
   corners too - so each is come up to on the level, with no dip before its door.

Each is checked in the workbench before the next.

### 7. Probe, map, biomes and timing

- **`scripts/probe/features.mjs`**, run across many seeds. It checks:
  - every camp's yard is level;
  - its buildings and props lie on the pads and do not overlap;
  - nothing stands on a lane, a road, a trail or out on water;
  - no tree grows inside the yard;
  - stumps lie only in the clearing;
  - every place has at least its fewest links, and none has more than its most;
  - every road entrance opens where its road arrives.

  It also reports the placement rate per biome, and the network's links by class.
- **Debug map:** the camp shows as a feature square with its name, and teleporting goes to its yard.
- **Biome odds:** snowy taiga, autumn wood and bluebell wood get a `features` list with the camp
  added beside settlements and "none". The odds are tuned by walking the world.
- **Timing:** measured in the startup probe. Layouts are measured in plan, models built only for
  what is drawn.
